For a long time, I treated play as something that happened after the real work was finished. The trouble was that the real work was never finished.

So I started reserving one hour each Friday for making something with no destination. No client, no polished outcome, and—most importantly—no requirement that the idea be good.

## A small weekly practice

Sometimes I cut shapes from coloured paper. Sometimes I redraw an object from memory. Sometimes I open an old sketchbook and continue an idea that stopped halfway down the page.

The hour has only three rules:

1. Start before deciding what the result should be.
2. Use what is already nearby.
3. Stop while it is still enjoyable.

The point is not to create portfolio work. It is to stay familiar with curiosity.

## Unfinished can be useful

A surprising number of these experiments quietly return months later. A colour pairing becomes the beginning of an identity. A strange paper shape solves a layout problem. A bad drawing reveals a good composition.

Not every idea needs to become something. Giving ideas room to remain unfinished makes the finished work feel more alive.
